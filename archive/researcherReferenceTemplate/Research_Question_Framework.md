# Research Question Framework

*Guidelines for creating effective research queries for LLM-powered research assistants*

---

## Core Structure of an Effective Research Query

Every effective research query shares these essential components:

1. **Clear Topic Definition**: Precisely define the subject matter with specific terminology
2. **Temporal Boundaries**: Specify a relevant timeframe (e.g., 2021-2025)
3. **Focus Areas**: Identify 2-4 specific aspects within the broader topic
4. **Expected Outputs**: Indicate what types of information are most valuable (benchmarks, code, etc.)
5. **Application Context**: Clarify how the research will be applied
6. **Practical Implementation**: Request concrete examples, code, or tools

---

## Why This Framework Creates Effective Research Queries

1. **Reduces Ambiguity**: Clear boundaries prevent the research from becoming too broad
2. **Ensures Relevance**: Temporal constraints focus on current information
3. **Facilitates Depth**: Specific focus areas allow for targeted exploration
4. **Prioritizes Actionability**: Emphasis on practical application makes research usable
5. **Enables Progressive Refinement**: Structured format supports iterative research
6. **Improves Reproducibility**: Clear parameters make research outcomes consistent

---

## Crafting Your Research Query

### Step 1: Define Your Core Question
- Begin with the specific problem or knowledge gap you're trying to address
- Use precise technical terminology
- Avoid overly broad topics

### Step 2: Add Temporal Context
- Specify the relevant timeframe (typically recent years)
- Consider whether historical context is important

### Step 3: Identify Key Dimensions
- List 2-4 specific aspects you want to explore
- Include technical metrics or methodologies where relevant

### Step 4: Specify Desired Outputs
- Clarify what types of information are most valuable
- Consider code examples, benchmarks, architectural patterns, etc.

### Step 5: Provide Application Context
- Explain how the research will be applied
- Include relevant constraints (hardware, regulatory, etc.)

### Step 6: Format for Clarity
- Structure as "Research Question — [Topic]" followed by "Researcher Task: [detailed description]"
- Use clear, imperative language

## Example Transformation

**Poor Query**: "Tell me about RAG systems"

**Improved Query**:
```
Research Question — Retrieval-Augmented Generation Architectures

Researcher Task:
Investigate recent developments (2022-2025) in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architectures for enhancing LLM accuracy and source attribution. Focus on innovations in retrieval methods, passage reranking, and integration with generative models. Identify performance benchmarks across factuality, hallucination reduction, and citation accuracy metrics. Provide examples of production-ready implementations, particularly those demonstrating scalability with large document collections. Where available, include architectural diagrams, code examples, and configuration patterns for systems built with open-source components.
```

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Overly Broad Scope**: Lack of specific focus areas dilutes research quality
2. **Missing Temporal Context**: Omitting timeframes may yield outdated information
3. **Unclear Outputs**: Failing to specify desired deliverables leads to generic responses
4. **Theoretical Focus**: Neglecting to request practical applications reduces usefulness
5. **Ambiguous Terminology**: Using imprecise language creates confusion
6. **Insufficient Context**: Not explaining how research will be applied limits relevance
